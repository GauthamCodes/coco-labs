## 3. Mathematics

**The correct state is (cell, incoming heading).** An edge's cost depends on
the direction of the previous move, so a cell's future cost depends on how
it was entered. Two arrivals at one cell with different headings are
different states. The two cell-only rules (finding 6: one heading per cell;
finding 8: strict-`<` relaxation) therefore discard an arrival that may
finish cheaper. This is true of both historical searches.

- **Cell-only loses optimality: MEASURED.**
  - `turn_trap`: one obstacle, 20 x 20.
  - At (6, 2) the arrivals heading north-west and heading north both have
    `g` = 3 + 3√2 + 0.1 = 7.3426, an exact tie.
  - The cell keeps the first one relaxed (north-west). Going on north then
    costs 1.1 instead of 1.0.
  - Both historical searches return 10.4426 against the optimum 10.3426.
  - Pinned in `test_isro.py::test_turn_trap_cell_only_search_loses_optimality`,
    down to `grid[6][2].direction == (-1, -1)`.
  - A strictly lower `g` within 0.1 can do the same in principle, but this
    fixture's mechanism is the tie.
- **Octile is admissible and consistent on the (cell, heading) model:
  PROVEN BY PROPERTY.**
  - Every edge costs at least its geometric length, and octile with √2 is
    consistent for geometric 8-connected costs. A penalty ≥ 0 only raises
    edge costs, and the goal sink costs 0 with h = 0.
  - Checked: `h(a) <= c(a,b) + h(b)` on every edge of 200 seeded maps (up to
    8 x 8, penalties 0 to 1, corner rule both ways), and `h(goal) = 0`.
    Consistency plus `h(goal) = 0` implies admissibility.
- **The corrected model is optimal: PROVEN BY PROPERTY.**
  - `HeadingGrid` searched by coco_lab's unchanged Dijkstra and A\*
    (octile) matches an independent networkx graph on 1,000 seeded maps.
  - That graph is built from the written-out rule, not from `HeadingGrid`.
  - The maps are up to 12 x 12, with penalties 0, 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 and the
    corner rule both ways. 934 have a path and 66 have none.
- **Ablation: PROVEN BY PROPERTY.** With penalty 0, `HeadingGrid`'s optimum
  equals the plain 8-connected `Grid`'s on 300 seeded maps.
- **Hypothesis (b), octile overestimating because diagonals cost 1: refuted
  by the source (SOURCE-VERIFIED).** Diagonals cost √2 (finding 3), and
  octile uses √2 (finding 4). The roadmap asked for the faulty variant
  anyway, so it was run as a counterfactual (M1b below).

## 4. A defect found on the way: the smoother can loop forever

This is SOURCE-VERIFIED and MEASURED.

- **The mechanism.**
  - `smooth_path` (l.309–322) jumps from `i` to the farthest `j` with
    `line_of_sight`.
  - `line_of_sight` (l.286–306) walks a Bresenham line that, for a diagonal
    step, visits one of the two orthogonal cells.
  - `neighbors` allows a diagonal past a blocked corner (finding 5).
  - When such a step is the only way forward from `path[i]`, no `j > i` is
    in sight. `j` falls to `i`, `path[i]` is appended, and `i` does not
    move: an infinite loop that grows a list.
- **Measured.**
  - A 3 x 3 map (`test_isro.py::test_the_historical_smoother_can_loop_forever`)
    never returns. A child process with a memory cap is still looping at
    the time limit.
  - `coco_lab.isro.historical.smoothing_terminates` replays the loop's
    control flow with the verbatim `line_of_sight`. Its prediction agreed
    with the verbatim smoother in **20 of 20** checked cases (10 hang,
    10 return; 10 x 10 maps, density 0.2).
  - Three of each are re-checked on every test run.
- **Frequency: MEASURED.** Share of historical Dijkstra paths the smoother
  could not finish, on 30 x 30 maps:

  | density | long trips | short trips |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.0 | 0 / 300 | 0 / 150 |
  | 0.1 | 141 / 300 | 30 / 150 |
  | 0.2 | 221 / 300 | 55 / 150 |
  | 0.3 | 259 / 298 | 81 / 149 |

- **Context: HYPOTHESIS.** The report's scenarios were sparse, like
  `startup.example.txt`'s three crates, and a hang only needs one crate at a
  corner the path cuts. That the historical runs never hit it is plausible
  but not known.
- **What the harness does.** It never calls the smoother on a path it cannot
  finish. It records `smoothing_terminates = False` and no step count.

## 5. The controlled experiment (MEASURED)

`docs/data/lab1b/isro_experiment.py`; summaries in
`docs/data/lab1b/isro_experiment.json`.

**Inputs, fixed per case.**
- One seeded 30 x 30 map.
- The same start and goal for every model.
- The simulator's move set and order, and corner cutting as its `neighbors`
  allows.
- Densities 0.0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3.
- *Long* regime: 300 maps per density, with start and goal anywhere.
- *Short* regime: 600 maps, with the goal within 6 cells of the start, since
  Table 1's ≈2.6 waypoints suggests short trips.

**Models.**

| model | what |
|---|---|
| M1 | historical `astar` / `dijkstra`, verbatim, penalty 0.1 (cell-only) |
| M1b | counterfactual hypothesis (b): coco_lab A\* (octile) vs Dijkstra with diagonals costing 1 |
| M2 | `HeadingGrid` with coco_lab A\* (octile) / Dijkstra, penalty 0.1: the (cell, heading) optimum |
| M3 | ablation: M1 and M2 with penalty 0.0 |

**Measurement.**
- Every returned path is priced by one function,
  `HeadingGrid.path_cost` at 0.1, whichever model found it.
- "Steps" is the simulator's `len(smooth_path(path))`.
- Time is the search call alone, with the harness instruments off, on this
  machine. It is excluded from the determinism hash.
- Two full runs gave the same hash, `c97ad0fc5e5bbc84…`.

**Results, long regime** (1,198 maps with a path, 2 without; every model
agreed on which):

| comparison | maps that differ | direction | size |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 A\* vs M1 Dijkstra, true cost | 148 / 1,198 | A\* higher 82, lower 66 | mean +0.007 %, range −1.99 % to +1.87 % |
| M1 A\* vs optimum (M2) | 201 / 1,198 | always higher | mean where it differs +0.67 %, max +2.37 % |
| M1 Dijkstra vs optimum | 184 / 1,198 | always higher | mean where it differs +0.69 %, max +2.22 % |
| M2 A\* vs M2 Dijkstra | **0 / 1,198** | — | — |
| M3 historical A\* / Dijkstra vs geometric optimum (penalty 0) | **0 / 1,198** each | — | — |
| M1b A\* vs Dijkstra, model cost (diagonals = 1) | 29 / 1,198 | always higher | max +11.1 % |
| M1b A\* vs Dijkstra, geometric length | 434 / 1,198 | A\* longer 360, shorter 74 | mean +1.72 %, mean where it differs +4.74 % |
| M1 "Steps", A\* / Dijkstra (568 maps both smoothable) | 10 / 568 | A\* more 4, fewer 6 | **ratio of means 0.9993** |
| M1 search time, Dijkstra / A\* | — | — | **4.47x** (means), **5.41x** (medians); second run 4.50x / 5.42x |

**Results, short regime** (599 with a path):
- M1 A\* and M1 Dijkstra costs differ on 29 maps.
- M1 is suboptimal on 42 (A\*) and 41 (Dijkstra) maps, max +2.85 %.
- M2 A\* equals M2 Dijkstra on all 599.
- "Steps" ratio of means **0.9966** (432 smoothable).
- Time ratio **2.19x** (means), **2.02x** (medians).

What this shows:
- The cell-only state costs the historical searches optimality on about one
  map in six, by up to about 2.4 %.
- It does NOT bias A\* against Dijkstra. The two historical searches differ
  in both directions and on average by nothing. The mechanism is real, but
  it does not produce a systematic "A\* is 4 % worse".
- Removing the penalty (M3) or putting the heading in the state (M2)
  removes every discrepancy.
- On the "Steps" metric the experiment reproduces **no** gap of the
  historical size or direction.
- The timing ratio is of the historical magnitude on long trips here.
