\bench{} turns a navigation result into something a reader can re-run in a browser and check against
a hash chain, rather than a number to be trusted. Pairing the deterministic model with the full ROS~2
stack on the same scenarios turns the model's main weakness, its simplicity, into something measured:
we can say where its verdicts carry over and where they do not. The any-angle planner that wins in the
model and fails under Nav2's controller shows why both evidence classes are needed. The suites, the
leaderboard, the export to ROS~2 nodes and the scripts that regenerate every number in this paper are
open source.
