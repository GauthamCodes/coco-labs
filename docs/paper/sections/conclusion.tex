\bench{} makes a navigation result something a reader can re-run in a browser and check against
a hash chain, rather than a number to trust. Pairing the deterministic model with the full ROS~2
stack on the same scenarios turns the model's main weakness, its simplicity, into something
measured: we can say where its verdicts carry over and where they do not, and the any-angle
planner that wins in the model and fails under Nav2's controller shows why both are needed.
The suites, the leaderboard, the export to ROS~2 nodes and the scripts that regenerate every
number in this paper are open source.
