\textbf{A kinematic model.} The browser model is differential-drive planar kinematics with
terrain following: no dynamics, contact forces, friction, wheel slip by default, or tipping.
Its verdicts are about algorithms on this model; \cref{sec:exp-gap} measures how far they carry
to ROS~2 in Gazebo, which is itself a simulation.

\textbf{No hardware.} No physical robot was used. Every result here is \evclass{model} or
\evclass{stack} (ROS~2 and Gazebo, simulated); nothing is a real-robot result.

\textbf{One robot, authored worlds.} All suites use one robot model and worlds authored in a
World Spec; there is no multi-robot interaction and no scanned real building.

\textbf{Scenario generation.} Scenarios are drawn from free space with a clearance margin and
must be connected; filtered suites keep scenarios whose reference A* path passes the filter. The
suites therefore under-represent starts in tight spots, unreachable goals and cases that A*
handles badly but other planners do not.

\textbf{Held-out scenarios.} The secret keeps the held-out set unpredictable and outside the
sandbox, but the set comes from the same generator as the public suite, so a method tuned to that
distribution is not caught. A submission that escaped both the Python guard and the container
could still falsify its own reported numbers; rounding and per-push sets limit what such a
submission can learn or leak, but do not make its numbers trustworthy. Review remains part of the
process.

\textbf{Simulated live stack and emulated phones.} The interactive ``Live Stack'' in the
application is simulated; the \evclass{stack} results in this paper are recorded Gazebo runs.
Phone results quoted anywhere in the project are on an emulated device unless stated otherwise.
