# COCO Depot — design notes (M6F.3)

`worlds/coco_depot_v1.yaml` (World Spec v3), generated by
`worlds/tools/make_coco_depot_v1.py`. A night-shift warehouse, 48 × 32 m (README
§5.9), world frame = map frame (`world_to_map` 0, 0), game resolution 0.1 m (a
490 × 330 grid with the 0.5 m margin). Evidence class: MODEL (the level is a
design; whether the Stack can run it is M6F.8, STACK). Tests:
`coco_lab/test/test_depot.py`.

The robot block is the COCO arena's, copied (a test asserts they agree). The only
grade in the level is the arena's bay ramp (run 2 m, rise 2 · tan 18° = 0.6498 m,
2.5 m wide): the one grade the Stack's robot has climbed. Every solid stands taller
than the LiDAR beam (0.2135 m), so the Stack's 2D scan sees what the model's map holds.

## The six zones, and what each makes visible

| Zone | What is there | What it makes visible |
|---|---|---|
| **Loading dock** (x 35.5–47.85, y 0–9.5) | Three dock drop zones on the south wall; a staging row of six loaded pallets (1.2 × 1.0 × 1.4 m) with **0.8 m gaps**; a pallet pair with a **0.4 m gap** (narrower than the robot's 0.44 m diameter) holding a can between them. | The local costmap and inflation: 0.8 m is above the 0.64 m the plan map can pass at 0.1 m, so a global planner threads the gaps and a local controller must hold the line. The 0.4 m gap is where "blocked" is taught: the can is in range, the right size and magnetic, and no collision-free pose puts it in the approach window. |
| **High-bay aisles** (x 16–31.4, y 15–28.8) | Twelve 6 m racks in six rows, back-to-back pairs, three 2.4 m aisles, a 2.4 m transverse gap; shelves at 0.25, 1.75, 3.25, 4.75 m. | Long corridors where the heuristic matters: A\* and greedy go straight down an aisle, Dijkstra and BFS flood every aisle first, and the transverse gap gives a shortcut a greedy search can miss. Objects on shelves are visible and out of reach. |
| **Mezzanine with a ramp** (deck x 36–47.7, y 24–31.7, 0.6498 m high; ramp x 34–36) | A platform deck with guard rails, reached only by the ramp on its west side. | Terrain: the model follows the ramp (height and pitch; MODEL · kinematic) and plans onto the deck. The Nav2 map, built from a flat 2D scan, sees only the silhouette and never plans onto it — the tiers' difference made visible, not hidden. |
| **Office block** (x 0–14, y 20–32) | Four rooms off a corridor; 0.9 m room doors, a 1.2 m entrance; desks with items on them. | Doorways: planners squeezing through 0.9 m (0.64 m passable at 0.1 m), the cost of going round the walls, frontier exploration room by room (M7's Fog Scout). Desk items are out of reach. |
| **Workshop maze** (x 0–14, y 0–18) | A 7 × 9 maze of 2 m cells (seeded recursive backtracker, seed 20261017) with 5 extra openings, 2 m walls, three 1.2 m entrances. | Dead ends and backtracking: BFS and Dijkstra explore the maze's dead ends, A\* with an octile heuristic is drawn toward the wall nearest the goal; the five loops give A\*, Dijkstra and greedy different answers. Graspable items sit in dead ends. |
| **Forklift lanes** (east–west y 10–13.5; north–south x 32.5–35) | Two forklifts (2.4 × 1.2 × 2.2 m) shuttling at 1.5 m/s; two walkers (0.5 × 0.5 × 1.75 m) crossing at 1.2 m/s. All are kinematic functions of time with collision bodies, phased so no two ever meet (checked over 600 s). | Moving obstacles: local planners (DWA, pure pursuit, MPPI) timing a crossing, minimum clearance, waiting versus going round (Rush Hour). In Gazebo they are models with collision bodies, so a controller cannot "arrive" through one (Lab 5's lesson). |

The dispatch floor (x 14.5–32, y 0–9.5) holds the start (23, 6), the dispatch drop zone,
and two packing benches.

The forklift and walker speeds and sizes are level design, not measured vehicles. A
Rush Hour score uses them as given. The maze seed, the rack and door dimensions, and the
pallet layout are also level design.

## Object catalog and grasp affordances

The envelope is never typed in the level. Grasp Assist (`coco_lab/coco_lab/grasp_assist.py`)
computes it from existing constants:

| Envelope number | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Approach window (ahead of `base_footprint`) | 0.151–0.1565 m | `coco_lab.grasp.WINDOW` = `coco_config.robot.approach_window()` (`GRASP_SELF_COLLISION_X` + margin, `GRASP_REACH_X_MAX`), pinned by `coco_lab_ros/test/test_sketch_constants.py` |
| Lateral limit | 10 mm | `coco_lab.grasp.MAX_LATERAL` = `coco_config.robot.GRASP_MAX_LATERAL` |
| Widest object the fingers have closed on | 32 mm | `max(coco_lab.grasp.TARGET_DIAMETER)` = the yellow target, `coco_config.robot.TARGETS` |
| Widths the Stack has picked | 20–32 mm | the four targets, `coco_config.robot.TARGETS` (12/12 fetches, C2-NAV.49) |
| Where the magnet binds | 30 mm below the top | `coco_lab.grasp.TARGET_HEIGHT` − `coco_lab.arm.TARGET_XZ[1]` (0.158 − 0.128); **ASSUMPTION** for objects other than the Stack's targets |
| Reach envelope (grasp-band height above the robot's floor) | 0.099–0.128 m (derived) | `coco_lab.arm.ik` reaching both window ends at the band and at `coco_lab.arm.HOVER_CLEARANCE` (0.07 m, `coco_config.robot.GRASP_HOVER_CLEARANCE`) above it; the Stack's 0.128 m is its top edge |
| Holding | magnet | two fingers and a magnet; the magnet does the holding (README §2) |

Placed objects (18): ten graspable (two in maze dead ends, two on the mezzanine deck,
two in high-bay aisles, two in offices, one on the dispatch floor, one at the dock),
and eight that teach a refusal. Each refusal object's first reason, in README order,
is tested:

| Object | Item, where | First reason |
|---|---|---|
| `bad_too_wide` | paint tin, dispatch floor | too wide |
| `bad_crate` | crate, dock floor | too wide (also out of reach, not magnetic) |
| `bad_too_low` | low tin, office floor | out of reach |
| `bad_on_shelf` | small can, rack C1 shelf 0.25 m | out of reach (band 0.378 m) |
| `bad_on_desk` | canister, office desk 0.75 m | out of reach (band 0.878 m) |
| `bad_on_bench` | large can, packing bench 0.9 m | out of reach (band 1.028 m) |
| `bad_not_magnetic` | plastic bottle, high-bay aisle 2 | not magnetic |
| `bad_blocked` | large can, in the 0.4 m gap between two pallets | blocked |

## Outputs

`tools/m6/gen_level.py worlds/coco_depot_v1.yaml --arena-res 0.1 --arena-res 0.05
--nav-res 0.05 --gz-check` writes the Arena world (0.1 m and 0.05 m), the Gazebo SDF (with
wedge STL, spawnable objects, `actors.json`), and the Nav2 map (0.05 m, as the Stack's
costmaps run) with derived params. Their checksums are committed in
`docs/v2/m6/data/coco_depot_v1_outputs.json`; the outputs themselves stay out of git.
